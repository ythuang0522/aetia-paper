\begin{abstract}
Distinguishing causative pathogens from incidental microbial detections remains a challenge in metagenomic diagnosis of respiratory infection. We developed \toolname (\toolexpansion), which combines language models and prespecified rules to integrate metagenomic, clinical, microbiological and published evidence. In a retrospective development cohort of 55 patients with suspected lower respiratory tract infection at three hospitals, \toolname achieved 71.6\% precision and 91.4\% recall against physician-adjudicated pathogens. Its F1 score, summarizing precision and recall, was 80.3\%, compared with 14.8\% for unfiltered sequencing reports, 50.0\% for multiplex polymerase chain reaction and galactomannan testing, and 45.5\% for culture. In a 41-patient subset, \toolname exceeded four language models directly prompted with the same data by 13.6--15.1 F1 points. Each organism's selection or exclusion was linked to the underlying evidence. These findings support integrating clinical context with metagenomic results for pathogen attribution; external validation and prospective evaluation are needed to establish clinical utility.
\end{abstract}

 \textbf{a}, Seven modality-specific language-model agents turn the record into structured evidence for each organism.
 \textbf{b}, Rules grade \mngs detections and conventional-test results, merge them into one candidate pool and hold back likely colonizers; a language-model safety review flags unpicked organisms but cannot select them. Two organisms are Picked, two Flagged and three held back.
 \textbf{c}, Each Picked or Flagged organism receives an evidence profile, not a decision: one verdict per PubMed article matched to the patient's site, host and timing; read count and dominance over the next organism; conventional tests by specimen class; and site relevance. Bars are schematic.
 \textbf{d}, Picked organisms are kept. A Flagged organism is added only if the same organism is isolated from a sterile site or tissue (here, a mycobacterium in pleural-fluid culture) or if literature, site and sequencing all agree (not met by the mould). The list is fixed before a language model writes the clinical rationale; the \textit{K.~pneumoniae} entry is drawn from a prototype vignette (Fig.~3e) and is not a delivered record. Criteria are defined in Methods and Supplementary Tables~1--4.}
