\subsection*{Supplementary Note 1. Missed-candidate safety-review prompt}
The review prompt is embedded in \texttt{upstream/tools/review\_missed\_mngs\_candidates.py} (\texttt{build\_prompt}). The model receives the review queue, deterministic output, hospital summary and \mngs specimen assignments and returns one of four categories: \texttt{review\_high\_priority}, \texttt{review\_context\_needed}, \texttt{review\_low\_specificity} or \texttt{omitted\_with\_reason}. It may not replace deterministic selections or upgrade an organism because of immunocompromise or protected status alone. Respiratory \textit{Candida} and generic yeasts are considered low-specificity without same-species invasive evidence, and different \textit{Candida} species are not treated as aliases. CMV, HSV and VZV are classified as reactivation without evidence of primary pulmonary disease; EBV is not considered a pulmonary candidate. Additional rules address non-dominant herpesvirus signals, oral and aspiration flora, water-borne Gram-negative bacteria, gastrointestinal pathogens, coagulase-negative staphylococci and corynebacteria. The output is a JSON object with Traditional-Chinese rationale fields.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 2. Direct-prompting baseline prompt}
File \texttt{LLM\_test/direct\_raw\_benchmark/prompt\_v1.txt} (SHA-256 prefix \texttt{961087b8acdf}), original in Traditional Chinese. English rendering:

\begin{quote}\small
You are an infectious-diseases clinical reviewer. Using only the single patient's data provided here, decide which microorganisms are most likely the clinical causative pathogens of the current pulmonary or lower-respiratory-tract infection episode.

This is the Direct-Raw baseline: do not use external search, PubMed, RAG, AETIA, any candidate pool or any earlier model result. The raw \mngs list may contain contamination, colonization, background organisms, low-level detections and organisms unrelated to the site of infection; ``detected'' does not mean ``pathogen''. Judge from the \mngs reads and the laboratory's control ranking, the specimen site, cultures, molecular tests, imaging, host status and timing. Respiratory \textit{Candida}, low-specificity environmental organisms, skin flora and latent or reactivated viruses must not be listed as the infection source merely because they were detected. A positive result from a non-pulmonary specimen must not be treated as a pulmonary source without a pulmonary link. An empty array means ``no record available'', not a negative test. You may select zero, one or several pathogens; do not force an answer. Put only the organisms you finally judge to be the infection source in \texttt{predicted\_pathogens}; if evidence is insufficient, put them in \texttt{excluded\_detected\_organisms} or \texttt{limitations}. Name pathogens at species level; do not merge different \textit{Candida} or other species. Output must follow the given JSON schema; supporting evidence, counter-evidence, reason and limitations should be short and auditable, and hidden reasoning must not be output.
\end{quote}

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 3. Case examples}
The cases in Fig.~4e,f originated from the 2026-01-17 presentation of the first pipeline version in a \kmuh intensive-care series. In case 1, \textit{Klebsiella pneumoniae} was dominant in the bronchoalveolar specimen (68,569 reads) and was selected in the presence of new bilateral consolidation, pleural effusions, increasing C-reactive protein and worsening hypoxaemia. \textit{Candida albicans} from the same specimen was classified as airway colonization because of low, non-dominant reads, repeated low-burden respiratory culture and the respiratory-\textit{Candida} guardrail. In case 2, \textit{Candida tropicalis} met the C3 direct-evidence criterion because several blood-culture sets grew the same species and the bronchoalveolar read burden was high.

The full record delivered for each organism contains: the disposition (selected or not) and the rule path that produced it; the value of modules A, A1, B, C and D; the per-modality findings recorded as support or opposition; the PubMed identifiers and judged spans of the case-matched articles; the specimen, site and timing used; and the SHA-256 hashes of the input files. \todo{Replace these prototype vignettes with the delivered \rfive records (selected organisms, rule paths, module results, audited explanation, reference labels) and obtain approval for case-level presentation.}

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 4. Version boundaries and replay}
The 55 reported \rfive decisions used rule version v19 (\path{mngs_deterministic_level_only_v19_low_specificity_taxonomy}). The recovered v19 scorer reproduced all saved scorer outputs from the stored summaries and ranked \mngs records. The current scorer, \vtwenty (\path{mngs_deterministic_level_only_v20_central_taxonomy_profile}), uses a central rule-based organism classifier, removes a retired language-model scoring branch and retains M1 for typical pneumonia bacteria with lower-respiratory culture or \filmarray support where v19 assigned M2. Replaying the same inputs reproduced all candidate names, selected sets, merge-stage selections and review-tier assignments. Candidate levels changed in 15 patients and selection order changed in five. The clinical-summary replay used current patient directories rather than a verified frozen agent snapshot; reconstructed hospital evidence differed from the stored summaries for three patients, without changing any reported selection. Sources: \path{docs/UPSTREAM_REPLAY_SUMMARY.json}, \path{docs/UPSTREAM_RECOVERY.md} and \path{docs/VERSIONS.md}.

\subsection*{Supplementary Note 6. Registry analysis of the sequencing report alone}
The shared registry contained adjudicated pathogen lists for 33 cases (Methods; \texttt{tables/make\_discordance.py}; \texttt{figures-source/discordance.csv}). \mngs reported 104 organisms, of which 38 (37\%) were adjudicated as causative and 66 (63\%) as colonizing, contaminating or incidental. The read-count distributions overlapped across five orders of magnitude. Read count ranked a causative organism above a non-causative organism in 61\% of pairs (AUC 0.61), with AUCs of 0.73 for bronchoalveolar lavage fluid and sputum and 0.56 for blood, cerebrospinal fluid and tissue. The largest count, 189,467 reads of \textit{Acinetobacter nosocomialis} in lavage fluid, was adjudicated as colonization despite culture growth. \textit{Pneumocystis jirovecii} with 7 reads in another lavage specimen was adjudicated as causative on the basis of a positive lavage PCR.

Ten species were causative in one patient and non-causative in another: cytomegalovirus, Epstein--Barr virus, herpes simplex virus 1, \textit{P.~jirovecii}, \textit{Klebsiella pneumoniae}, \textit{Pseudomonas aeruginosa}, \textit{A.~nosocomialis}, \textit{Burkholderia cenocepacia}, \textit{Candida albicans} and \textit{Candida tropicalis}. The list includes airway colonizers and recognized pathogens from multiple organism classes. Species identity and a uniform read threshold did not replace patient-specific clinical context, particularly for non-sterile respiratory specimens.

Evidence from other modalities improved discrimination. An exact-organism conventional test corroborated 47\% of causative detections and 18\% of non-causative detections. Seventeen of 55 adjudicated pathogens (31\%) were absent from the \mngs report of the sequenced specimen; 16 (94\%) were identified by another test, including culture in eight, PCR in five, serum galactomannan in two and IgM serology in one (\texttt{figures-source/missed\_by\_mngs.csv}). Fourteen of these tests were collected within two days of the \mngs specimen, and the longest interval was five days. Eleven pathogens were identified in a different specimen type, and five were identified in the same specimen type by a different testing principle.
