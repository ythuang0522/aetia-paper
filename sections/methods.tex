\subsection*{Ethics and study oversight}
% direct-prompting de-identification: LLM_test/direct_raw_benchmark/direct_raw_runner.py
% (replace_free_text_dates:258, exact_date_hits:337, store=False in the request:595-608)
This retrospective study analysed existing clinical records and \mngs reports; patients were not contacted, and pipeline outputs were not used in care. The institutional review boards of Kaohsiung Medical University Hospital (\kmuh; \todo{protocol number}), Taipei Veterans General Hospital (\tvgh; \todo{protocol number}) and Tri-Service General Hospital (\tsgh; \todo{protocol number}) approved the study and waived informed consent for retrospectively collected, de-identified data. Direct identifiers were removed before records were sent to a commercial language-model service through an application programming interface whose terms exclude training on submitted data, and reference labels were withheld from all model inputs. In the direct-prompting payloads, calendar dates were replaced by days relative to the \mngs specimen, a check blocked any payload that retained an exact date, and provider-side storage of responses was disabled. \todo{IRB coverage of external API use; de-identification standard; date handling in the \toolname stages; zero data retention.} \toolname was evaluated as research software returning candidate pathogens for physician review.

\subsection*{Study design and participants}
This retrospective, multicentre diagnostic-accuracy study is reported according to STARD 2015\cite{bossuyt2015stard} and TRIPOD-LLM\cite{gallifant2025tripod} \todo{attach both checklists}. It included patients who underwent \mngs for suspected lower respiratory tract infection at three tertiary centres in Taiwan (\kmuh, \tvgh and \tsgh) between August 2023 and September 2025. The index test was \toolname and the reference standard the adjudicated causative-pathogen list. Comparators were conventional read-outs taken at face value and four general-purpose language models prompted directly.

The hospitals' shared registry held 105 \mngs specimens (\kmuh, 51; \tvgh, 35; \tsgh, 19) with demographics, comorbidities, microbiology, \mngs reports, treatment changes and outcomes (Table~\ref{tab:cohort}). Membership of the two evaluation cohorts was recorded in versioned manifests (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:ed1}). The primary cohort comprised 55 patients (\kmuh, 30; \tvgh and \tsgh, 25) with a complete standardized record, a per-specimen \mngs report and at least one adjudicated pathogen at a pulmonary site. Patients were excluded if chest imaging, blood counts or microbiology were missing or too poorly documented for multimodal evaluation, or if the final adjudicated diagnosis was not pneumonia (upper respiratory tract infection alone, infection at another site or a noninfectious condition). The estimates therefore apply to patients in whom lower respiratory tract infection was ultimately confirmed, a population that cannot be identified when the test is ordered (Discussion). \todo{Numbers excluded per reason and alternative diagnoses; whether exclusions preceded model inference; reasons for the two-hospital steps 54 $\rightarrow$ 48 $\rightarrow$ 25.} The direct-prompting cohort comprised the 30 \kmuh patients and the 11 \tvgh and \tsgh patients whose records were complete in every section ($n=41$).

\subsection*{Metagenomic sequencing and conventional microbiology}
\mngs was ordered by the treating physicians and performed by one clinical laboratory (Asia Pathogenomics, New Taipei City, Taiwan) with a published workflow\cite{chou2025investigating,takahashi2026diagnostic}: nucleic-acid extraction without host depletion; 75-bp single-end sequencing on an Element Biosciences AVITI system (about 40 million reads per sample); quality filtering\cite{bolger2014trimmomatic,schmieder2011quality}; removal of reads aligning to T2T-CHM13\cite{nurk2022complete,li2013aligning}; and alignment to a curated microbial database, with organisms reported when their normalized abundance exceeded ten times the highest value in a concurrent negative control (Supplementary Methods). Specimens were mainly bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (60\%) and blood (23\%). \toolname used only the clinical report (reported organisms, read counts and control-subtracted ranks), not raw reads.

Culture, the BioFire \filmarray Pneumonia Panel\cite{buchan2020practical}, the Platelia \textit{Aspergillus} galactomannan assay\cite{hung2021investigating} and targeted molecular tests were performed in each hospital's accredited laboratory and taken as reported (Supplementary Methods). Within the pipeline, a \filmarray bacterial target contributes evidence in proportion to its semi-quantitative bin, and galactomannan is positive at an index of $\ge$1.0 in lavage fluid and $\ge$0.5 in serum\cite{donnelly2020revision}.

\subsection*{Reference standard}
% label revisions: LLM_test/direct_raw_benchmark/rescore_gold_corrections_20260912.py (rescoring of frozen predictions)
A committee of three infectious-diseases physicians from the participating centres adjudicated each patient, as in clinical \mngs studies\cite{wilson2019clinical,miller2019laboratory}. It reviewed the presentation, imaging, blood counts, inflammatory markers, conventional microbiology, \mngs report, clinical course and response to treatment. It then classified every organism detected by any method as a causative pathogen of the current episode or as a colonizer, contaminant, reactivated virus or bystander, using clinical context for organisms that culture cannot confirm. Patients had one to eight causative pathogens. \todo{Decision rule and resolution of disagreement; independent labelling and $\kappa$; timing relative to pipeline development.} Two label revisions affecting four patients were made against the source adjudication documents after every method's predictions had been stored, and all methods were rescored without re-running any model. \todo{Who revised the labels; whether blind to predictions.} Labels were available only to the evaluation scripts. Because the committee saw the \mngs report, the reference standard was not independent of the index test (Discussion).

\subsection*{Data extraction and standardization}
% upstream/core/llm_parser.py (send_to_llm: model="gpt-5", temperature=1, 114-115); frontend/pipeline.py:260
Rule-based parsers split the \kmuh per-patient workbooks and the \tvgh and \tsgh master workbook into clinical sections and extracted text from case-presentation slides. Content present only in images was transcribed from rendered slides, which were linked to registry cases by patient, date and file metadata. A language model (GPT-5, OpenAI; temperature 1) converted each section to a fixed schema for nine domains: admission diagnosis, underlying conditions, complete blood count, other laboratory values, cultures, \filmarray, galactomannan, molecular microbiology and imaging. The \mngs report was the tenth input. Observations were restricted to a window anchored on the \mngs specimen, records without a parsable time were excluded, and hospital-specific identifiers prevented collisions between institutions.

\subsection*{Overview of \toolname}
\toolname analyses one patient at a time in five stages (Fig.~\ref{fig:overview}): language-model extraction of evidence by modality; a rule-based initial selection; a language-model safety review of unselected organisms; evidence assessment of the retained candidates and a rule-based final selection; and a clinical rationale written after the list is fixed. Language models extract or assess evidence; version-controlled rules make every selection. Each model task used a fixed prompt and output schema (Supplementary Table~5). For every organism considered, the pipeline recorded the evidence for and against selection, the rule that determined its disposition and SHA-256 hashes of the inputs.

\subsection*{Modality-specific evidence extraction}
% upstream/tools/analyze_llm_agent.py (agent specifications:56-111, DEFAULT_MODEL:27); upstream/tools/run_mngs_to_specimen_agent.py; upstream/core/summarize_agent.py
Seven language-model agents (GPT-5) each received one part of the record, without access to the other agents or the labels: imaging; clinical history and underlying conditions; blood counts and other laboratory values; galactomannan; \filmarray and other molecular microbiology; culture; and the \mngs report, whose agent assigned each organism to its source specimen and anatomical site.\todo{The code runs five clinical agents plus an \mngs-to-specimen agent with a deterministic alternative (\path{analyze_llm_agent.py:56-111}); reconcile, or state the configuration of the reported runs.} The host agent mapped immunosuppression, malignancy, corticosteroid exposure, renal failure and related factors to prespecified vulnerability categories. The imaging agent graded the likelihood and pattern of infection. Imaging was used as context, never as organism confirmation, which required culture, \filmarray, galactomannan or molecular evidence. A rule-based summary then recorded, for each organism, the support from each modality (support, no support or unknown), the anatomical alignment of its specimen and the host context. Severe host vulnerability widened consideration of borderline organisms but could not by itself cause selection.

\subsection*{Initial pathogen selection}
% upstream/tools/rank_mngs_candidate_microbes.py; upstream/tools/deterministic_mngs_max_scorer.py
% (typical_pneumonia_hospital_mngs_rule:567, hospital_only_candidates:1106, guardrail omits:1318-1366)
Each reported organism was described by its read count, relative abundance in the run, control-subtracted rank, dominance within the specimen and the anatomical relevance of that specimen (Supplementary Table~1). Repeated detections were resolved by rank and read burden, and names were normalized with a fixed synonym dictionary that did not merge distinct species. The scorer combined these features with specimen alignment, direct hospital-test support and host context to assign one of five ordered causative levels (Supplementary Table~2). Strong support required a favourable control-subtracted rank with high abundance or within-specimen dominance. Intermediate signals could be strengthened by exact-organism evidence from another test or by pathogen-specific rules, and weak or anatomically discordant signals were downgraded.

Guardrails addressed respiratory \textit{Candida}, oral and aspiration flora, skin and environmental organisms, reactivated or low-specificity viruses and weak non-dominant detections (Supplementary Table~3). Strongly supported organisms were selected. Borderline organisms needed compatible clinical context, direct microbiological support or a pathogen-specific rule, never host vulnerability alone, and respiratory \textit{Candida} always required evidence of invasive infection. When nothing qualified, the strongest candidate that passed the exclusion rules was reported as the best available. An organism absent from the \mngs report could be selected on exact-species culture, \filmarray, galactomannan or targeted molecular evidence from a relevant specimen. IgM serology alone was insufficient: \textit{Chlamydophila} or \textit{Mycoplasma pneumoniae} supported only by IgM was retained for contextual review.

\subsection*{Safety review of unselected organisms}
% upstream/tools/review_missed_mngs_candidates.py (DEFAULT_MODEL:22, build_prompt); run_full_pipeline.py:351; examples/full_pipeline_config.template.json (missed_review:
% gpt-5, reasoning medium); post-review rules: upstream/tools/merge_deterministic_max_with_missed_review.py
A language-model review (GPT-5, reasoning effort medium) examined the unselected organisms, given the deterministic result, the structured hospital evidence and the specimen assignments. It could not remove or reorder a selection. Its prompt stated that immunocompromise or membership of a clinically important group was insufficient on its own and restated the rules for colonization, reactivation, oral and environmental flora and \textit{Candida} (Supplementary Note~1). Rules then standardized names and removed candidates supported only by anatomically irrelevant specimens or weak context. The review could therefore enlarge the set sent to evidence assessment but could not add an organism to the final list.

\subsection*{Evidence assessment and final selection}
% RAG_re/rag_re/pubmed.py, rules.py; RAG_re_casefit/rag_re_casefit/case_card.py, rules.py; RAG_re_clinical/rag_re_clinical/clinical_rules.py
% (function:line detail in the Supplementary Methods comment); final rule: RAG_re_casefit/rag_re_casefit/rescue_evaluation.py (R5_A1_UNION_AND_B_LOOSE:423; rule text:533)
Each initial selection and each candidate retained by the review was assessed on five dimensions (Supplementary Table~4 and Supplementary Methods). For the literature dimension (module A), PubMed was searched for the organism with lower-respiratory-tract terms (publications to 30 June 2026; up to ten articles), and a language model (GPT-5.6 Luna) judged each abstract for human evidence, with a traceable excerpt, that the exact species causes infection at the relevant site. With too few assessable articles, the module abstained. For case matching (module A1), the model compared the same articles with a label-blind patient summary that omitted selections, read counts, culture and PCR results. Rules graded each comparison as a strong or partial match, a mismatch or insufficient evidence; colonization, a conflicting site or syndrome, non-human evidence or broader-taxon evidence could not support rescue. Sequencing strength (module B) captured absolute burden and relative prominence in the specimen. Direct microbiological evidence (module C) was graded by assay specificity and anatomical relevance, highest for exact-species detection at a sterile site or in tissue; respiratory \textit{Candida} culture alone did not count. Anatomical coherence (module D) was recorded for audit, and an explicit anatomical mismatch could not be overridden. Modules B--D were computed alongside A1, not as sequential filters.

The final rule (\rfive) kept every initial selection and added an unselected organism by either of two routes: exact-species evidence from a sterile site or tissue, or a prespecified level of case match with anatomical alignment and either high absolute burden or strong relative prominence (thresholds in Supplementary Table~4). Rescued organisms were appended after the initial selections without ranking. Failing both routes was recorded as insufficient evidence for selection, not as evidence against infection.

\subsection*{Clinical rationale and delivery}

After selection, a language model (GPT-5.6 Luna) wrote one rationale per selected organism from the fixed decision, which it was told was immutable, and from candidate-specific microbiology within two days of the \mngs specimen\todo{template default; take the window from the run manifest}. Automated checks required exactly one entry per selected organism, no organism outside the list and every required field; a failure blocked delivery. The delivered record gave the disposition of every organism detected by any method, with each statement linked to its structured evidence (Fig.~\ref{fig:explain}). For a selected organism, it gave the route, supporting and opposing findings, literature, sequencing evidence, specimen site and timing. For a non-selected organism, it gave the exclusion rule or unmet criterion and stated that non-selection does not exclude infection.

\subsection*{Comparators}
% direct prompting: LLM_test/direct_raw_benchmark/direct_raw_runner.py (request:585-617, stored fields:696-715, --max-output-tokens:777); prompt_v1.txt
Conventional read-outs were scored at face value, without reinterpretation: every organism on the \mngs report, every positive \filmarray or galactomannan target and every organism cultured within the evidence window. \todo{Check against the scoring script; handling of normal flora, no growth and pending results.}

To test whether performance came from the pipeline or from access to a language model alone, four general-purpose models (\gptsol, \gptluna, \gptterra and \gptastra; OpenAI) were prompted directly in the 41-patient cohort. Each received one request containing the nine standardized sections and the \mngs report (organisms, read counts, control-subtracted ranks and specimen types), without identifiers or any field derived from the reference standard or from pipeline decisions. The Traditional Chinese prompt cast the model as an infectious-diseases reviewer and stressed that detection does not establish causation. It named colonization, anatomical site, \textit{Candida}, environmental organisms and reactivation as concerns and allowed zero or more species-level pathogens. It required supporting and opposing evidence, excluded organisms and limitations (Supplementary Note~2). Models were queried once per patient (medium reasoning effort, strict JSON output schema, at most 4,000 output tokens, no tools, retrieval or conversation history), and responses were stored with their hashes and model identifiers before scoring. After a correction to the linkage between patients and records, 22 patients were re-queried under unchanged settings (Supplementary Methods); no response was edited.

\subsection*{Outcome measures and scoring}
% LLM_test/direct_raw_benchmark/evaluate_direct_raw.py (FROZEN_SYNONYMS:12, canonical:59, summarize:76, per-patient sets:238-243)
The unit of evaluation was the (patient, organism) pair. Names were normalized with a fixed synonym table (for example, CMV to human cytomegalovirus) and compared as sets at exact-species level. Genus and syndrome labels did not match, so \textit{Candida} against \textit{Candida tropicalis} gave one false positive and one false negative. The primary outcomes were micro-averaged precision, recall and F1. Secondary outcomes were patient-level macro-averages (an empty prediction scoring zero precision), the proportions of patients with at least one, all or exactly the reference pathogens, and the number of patients with no prediction (Supplementary Note~5). The selection made by the initial rules alone was scored in the same way, isolating the organisms added by the final rule. Because both cohorts required an adjudicated pathogen, precision does not capture false positives in pathogen-negative patients. Rationales were checked for completeness and for consistency with the decision but were not rated for usefulness or faithfulness \todo{reader study}.

Four exploratory analyses, not used to develop or evaluate the rules, describe the evidence available before integration: the concordance of sequencing with conventional microbiology (Supplementary Note~6), the composition and timing of the clinical record (Fig.~\ref{fig:explain}a--d), physiological trajectories (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:ed3}), and treatment changes and outcomes (Extended Data Fig.~\ref{fig:ed2}). \subsection*{Statistics and reproducibility}
No statistical method was used to predetermine sample size; the cohorts included every eligible registry patient when the datasets were finalized. All methods were evaluated on the same records, so there was no randomization. Labels were withheld from the language-model stages and read by the evaluation scripts only after predictions were stored. The rule developers, however, had access to the labels and no patients were held out, so the reported performance is a development-set result (Discussion). The clinical records are not public and cannot have been used to train the evaluated models.

Because organisms cluster within patients, 95\% confidence intervals for the micro-averaged metrics were estimated by resampling patients (10,000 bootstrap replicates, percentile intervals). Differences in F1 from each comparator were estimated by paired bootstrap, and exact-set agreement was compared by McNemar's exact test. The comparison with the \mngs report at face value was primary; the other six comparisons were secondary and unadjusted for multiplicity. Interval widths are reported in place of a power calculation, and results are also given separately for \kmuh and for \tvgh and \tsgh. Rule-based stages were exactly reproducible. Language-model stages were run once for the reported results, and run-to-run variability was estimated from repeat runs with unchanged inputs: the range of F1 and the proportion of patients with identical selections. \todo{Compute the intervals, tests, stratified metrics, ablation and repeat runs (state $n$); until then, metrics are point estimates.}
