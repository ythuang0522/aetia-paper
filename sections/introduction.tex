Lower respiratory tract infection remains the infection most often treated without a microbiological diagnosis. In the largest prospective aetiological study of community-acquired pneumonia requiring admission, no pathogen was identified in 62\% of patients despite exhaustive conventional testing\cite{jain2015community}, and in the intensive care unit, where prior antibiotic therapy reduces culture yield and the differential diagnosis includes fungi, viruses and opportunistic pathogens, the yield is lower still. Metagenomic next-generation sequencing (\mngs) of respiratory fluid or plasma was introduced to close this gap: a single assay that sequences all nucleic acid in the specimen and can, in principle, detect any bacterium, fungus, virus or parasite without a prior hypothesis\cite{chiu2019clinical,simner2018understanding}. Its diagnostic value has been demonstrated in meningoencephalitis\cite{wilson2019clinical}, bloodstream infection\cite{blauwkamp2019analytical,hogan2021clinical} and lower respiratory tract infection\cite{charalampous2019nanopore,langelier2018integrating,chen2020clinical,diao2022metagenomics}, and it is now ordered routinely for critically ill patients in many hospitals in Asia\cite{miao2018microbiological,han2019mngs}.

The difficulty has moved from detection to interpretation. Because \mngs is hypothesis-free, it reports everything: the upper-airway flora that every bronchoalveolar lavage carries\cite{charlson2011topographical,dickson2016microbiome}, reagent and environmental contaminants\cite{salter2014reagent}, latent herpesviruses that reactivate under critical illness, and \textit{Candida} species that colonize the airway in almost all patients receiving mechanical ventilation. The cost of that comprehensiveness is measurable in published series. Across 511 clinical specimens, \mngs reached a sensitivity of 50.7\% for infectious disease but a specificity of 85.7\%\cite{miao2018microbiological}; in a prospective bronchoalveolar lavage cohort it identified a definite or probable pathogen in 65\% of patients with lower respiratory tract infection, as compared with 20\% by culture, yet reached a specificity of 75.4\% and a positive predictive value of 78.5\% against a composite reference standard\cite{chen2020clinical}. Separating colonization from infection is identified as the central unresolved challenge in systematic assessments of the method\cite{simner2018understanding,diao2022metagenomics}. Laboratories mitigate this with read thresholds and no-template-control subtraction\cite{miller2019laboratory,schlaberg2017validation}, but a threshold cannot distinguish a \textit{Klebsiella} with tens of thousands of reads in a patient with new bilateral consolidation and rising C-reactive protein is a pathogen, whereas a \textit{Candida albicans} with similar reads in the same specimen is not. That judgement requires the culture and multiplex polymerase chain reaction (PCR) results, the galactomannan, the imaging, the blood counts, coexisting conditions and immunosuppression, and the timing of each test relative to the specimen, and it requires knowledge of what each organism does at that site in that kind of host. In practice it is made by an infectious diseases physician reviewing a dozen separate reports, and it is neither standardized nor recorded.

Sequencing does not retire the conventional tests, because the two fail in different directions. Culture alone yields a viable isolate and therefore phenotypic antimicrobial susceptibility, on which guideline-directed de-escalation depends\cite{metlay2019diagnosis,kalil2016management}, whereas \mngs reports nucleic acid whether or not the organism is viable and resistance inferred from sequence is not yet a substitute\cite{chiu2019clinical,simner2018understanding}. Multiplex PCR panels return a result within an hour, against thresholds fixed at validation\cite{buchan2020practical}. Antigen assays measure something sequencing never sees: galactomannan is a mycological criterion for invasive fungal disease in its own right\cite{donnelly2020revision}, and antibody responses identify organisms whose nucleic acid may never be abundant in the airway. Imaging establishes that pneumonia exists at all and where it is; the guideline definition of community-acquired pneumonia requires a radiographic infiltrate, not a positive microbiological test\cite{metlay2019diagnosis}. Two limits are structural rather than technical. First, a specimen reports only its own compartment, so an organism walled off in an empyema or abscess, or confined to blood or tissue, is absent from lavage fluid however deeply that fluid is sequenced. Second, detectability is a window: intermittent bacteraemia, a resolving viraemia and nucleic acid already cleared by effective therapy are each invisible to a specimen taken outside it, which is why specimen type, specimen selection and timing govern the yield of the assay\cite{gu2019clinical,miller2022role,schlaberg2017validation}. For these conditions and organisms \mngs will return false negatives at any sequencing depth, and the finding that corrects the record comes from a culture bottle, an antigen result or a chest scan. Causative diagnosis therefore rests on no single test but on the intersection of all of them, which makes it a multimodal problem before it is a sequencing problem.

Integrating that many sources, for every organism on a long report, is the task for which large language models are an obvious candidate. They encode clinical knowledge\cite{singhal2023large}, produce credible differential diagnoses from case vignettes\cite{mcduff2025towards,tu2025towards} and have been tested as consultants in infectious diseases\cite{maillard2024chatbot}. But performance on licensing examinations and curated vignettes has not transferred to realistic clinical work: across 2,400 real patient cases, current models diagnosed significantly worse than physicians, followed neither diagnostic nor treatment guidelines and could not interpret laboratory results\cite{hager2024evaluation}. The models also give no account of which test tipped the decision, cannot be audited for whether they used a finding they should not have seen, and change their answer from one run to the next\cite{ji2023survey}. Multi-agent prompting frameworks\cite{tang2024medagents,kim2024mdagents,qiu2024llm} and retrieval augmentation\cite{lewis2020retrieval,xiong2024benchmarking} address parts of this, but a pipeline that will sit between a sequencing laboratory and a prescriber needs more than a better prompt. It needs decisions that are reproducible, evidence that is traceable to a source rather than to a post-hoc account of the model's own behaviour\cite{ghassemi2021false}, a guarantee that the reference standard never leaks into the reasoning, a form of leakage that is a frequent cause of inflated performance in machine-learning-based science\cite{kapoor2023leakage}, and a clear separation between what the model selected and how it later explained the selection, because a model's stated reasoning need not be the reasoning that produced its answer\cite{turpin2023language}.

Here we describe \toolname (\toolexpansion), a pipeline built to those requirements and evaluated on patients with suspected lower respiratory tract infection at three medical centres in Taiwan. Single-modality language-model agents extract structured evidence, a deterministic scorer and pre-specified guardrails select organisms, case-matched PubMed literature can rescue a candidate the rules left out, and a rationale is written only after the selection is frozen. \toolname identifies causative pathogens more accurately than the raw \mngs report or any single conventional read-out, reduces the number of organisms presented to the physician, and outperforms frontier language models prompted directly with the identical de-identified data, chiefly through recall. Every decision replays byte-for-byte from frozen intermediates and carries its rule path and the hashes of the inputs that produced it. \toolname is research software whose output is a candidate list for clinical review, not a diagnosis; its contribution is to show that structured, literature-grounded integration, rather than end-to-end prompting, is what makes \mngs interpretable at the bedside.
